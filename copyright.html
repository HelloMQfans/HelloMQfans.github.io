Copyright & Public Domain

Historical Literary Works in the Public Domain

The Narrating Mime presents narrations of selected historical literary works that are in the public domain in the United States.

In this website, the term historical literary work refers to the work created by the original historical author. For example, The Problem of the Lost Radium was written by Jacques Futrelle and published in 1906.

The public-domain status discussed on this website applies to the historical literary work itself. The Narrating Mime does not claim ownership of the historical literary work or of the authorship of its original historical author.

Our 2026 Narration Recordings

The narration recordings presented on this website and on The Narrating Mime YouTube channel are newly created audio performances of historical literary works.

The historical literary work and the 2026 narration recording are two different things. The public-domain status of the historical literary work does not mean that every modern recording, adaptation, edition, or other version of that work is automatically public domain.

The Narrating Mime claims rights, where applicable, in the newly created narration recordings and other material that has been independently created for this project.

Spanish Translations

Some historical literary works presented by The Narrating Mime are also made available in Spanish translation.

A Spanish translation is a newly created version of the historical literary work. It should not be confused with the historical literary work itself.

A translation may contain new authorship contributed by the translator. Any such rights apply to the newly created translation material and do not change the public-domain status of the underlying historical literary work.

Website and Project Materials

The Narrating Mime also contains materials created specifically for this project. These may include website text, descriptions, graphics, page design, organization, narration-related materials, and other project content.

These project materials are separate from the historical literary works presented on the website. The fact that a historical literary work is in the public domain does not automatically place these newly created project materials in the public domain.

Individual Works and Sources

Public-domain status can depend on the particular work, its publication history, jurisdiction, and other legal circumstances. For this reason, the public-domain status of each historical literary work is considered individually.

Where appropriate, individual narration pages identify the historical author, literary work, publication information, source text, translation, and other relevant information.

About This Information

This page provides general information about the distinction between historical literary works, newly created narration recordings, translations, and other project materials presented by The Narrating Mime.

It is not intended to provide legal advice or to make a determination about the copyright status of every work in every country.

Copyright laws differ between countries. A historical literary work that is in the public domain in the United States may not necessarily have the same status in another jurisdiction.
