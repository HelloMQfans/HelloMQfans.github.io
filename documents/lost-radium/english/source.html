The Problem of the Lost Radium

Chapter 1

ONE ounce of the chemical element: radium!

Within his open palm Professor Dexter held practically the world’s entire supply of the chemical element radium, and due to its seemingly inexhaustible energy, is one of the greatest scientific riddles.

As far as it was known, there were only a few more grams in existence: four in the Curie laboratory in Paris, two in Berlin, two in St. Petersburg, one at Leland Stanford University and one in London. All the remainder were here, in the Yarvard laboratory, a tiny mass lumped on a small box of steel.

Gazing at this vast concentrated power Professor Dexter was a little awed and a little appalled at the responsibility which had suddenly devolved upon him, naturally enough with this culmination of a project which he had cherished for months.

Briefly this had been to gather into one cohesive whole the many particles of the precious substance scattered over the world for the purpose of elaborate experiments as to the practicability of its motive power or energy source. Now here it was. Its value, based on scarcity of supply, was incalculable. Millions of dollars would not replace it.

Minute portions had come from the four quarters of the globe, in each case by special messenger, and each separate gram had been heavily insured by Lloyd’s Insurance Company of London, at a staggering premium. It was only after months of labor, backed by the influence of the great University of Yarvard in which he held the chair of physics, that Professor Dexter had been able to accomplish his purpose.
